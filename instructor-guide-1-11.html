Repetition & Proximity

Creating relationships through space and consistency
The Big Idea
Two principles, one purpose — creating relationships. Repetition builds unity across a design by repeating visual elements; proximity builds meaning within a design by grouping related elements in space. Together they let a viewer understand what belongs with what, without reading a single word.
What you're really teaching
  • Proximity is a Gestalt principle: elements near each other are perceived as related, elements far apart as unrelated. Structure is communicated through space alone — perception, not instruction.
  • The classic failure is equal spacing. On an event flyer where a session's own details are spaced the same as the gaps between sessions, the information is all there but the relationships are invisible. The gap between groups must be bigger than the gap within them.
  • Intra-group versus inter-group spacing. Keep related items tight (intra), separate groups wide (inter). The contrast between those two spacings is what makes structure visible.
  • Repetition is proximity's companion. Consistent type, color, spacing, and shape across the groups makes the whole design unified and recognizable — and is the foundation of brand recognition.
  • A repetition system is small and strict: for example two typefaces, three colors, two spacing intervals, one shape language — defined first, then applied everywhere without deviation.
Scenarios & hooks to open the room
The evenly-spaced flyer

Six sessions, everything equally spaced. You can't tell which time goes with which session. The canonical proximity failure.

Tighten and separate

Same flyer — group each session tight, widen the gaps between them. The structure appears instantly.

The three-second count

Flash a layout for three seconds. Can students count the sessions without reading? If not, the proximity contrast is too weak.

The style system

Two fonts, three colors, two spacing intervals applied everywhere. Watch unity emerge from repetition alone.

Teaching the lesson — step by step
Hook: count the sessions in three seconds 6 min

Flash an evenly-spaced list; students can't count it. A proximity failure, felt immediately.

Proximity as Gestalt 10 min

Near equals related. The gap between groups must exceed the gap within — intra versus inter spacing.

Fix a proximity failure live 8 min

Tighten intra-group, widen inter-group. The structure snaps into view with no new content.

Repetition and the repetition system 10 min

Define a small system — two fonts, three colors, two gaps, one shape — and apply it. Unity from consistency.

Set up the combined exercise 6 min

Repetition system plus proximity grouping on a five-session program, verified by the three-second test.

Close on real information design 5 min

Students critique one real menu or schedule for both proximity and repetition.

Facilitating the Field Manual — Repetition System + Proximity Grouping
Running the exercise

Students define a repetition system and apply it to a multi-session program that lives or dies on proximity grouping.

  • Define the repetition system first — two typefaces, three colors, two spacing intervals (a tight intra gap and a wide inter gap), one shape language — before laying anything out.
  • Build real structure: an event program of at least five sessions, each with title, time, location, and speaker.
  • Apply proximity — group each session's details tight, separate sessions with the wide gap — so the sessions are instantly distinguishable.
  • Apply repetition — identical treatment for every title, time, and session structure, with no deviation.
  • Run the three-second test: can a viewer count the sessions without reading? If not, widen the inter-group gap until they can.
The Assignment & Deliverable — Repetition + Proximity Study
What good work looks like

An event program on a defined repetition system, grouped so the session count reads in a three-second glance.

  • Meets the bar: a clear contrast between intra- and inter-group spacing and a consistently applied system.
  • Exceeds: the layout passes the three-second count test cleanly and the repetition system is documented — fonts, colors, and gaps named.
  • Redirect: sessions bleeding into each other means the inter-group gap must exceed the intra-group gap.
Watch for
  • Equal spacing everywhere. The relationship-killer — and the whole point of the lesson.
  • A system defined but not followed. Any deviation breaks the unity repetition is meant to create.
  • Grouping with boxes and lines instead of space. Let proximity do the work before adding dividers.
  • Intra-group gaps as wide as inter-group gaps. The contrast between them is what communicates structure.
Discussion questions
  • Why can equal spacing make information unreadable even when every fact is present?
  • Where have you seen proximity done well — a menu, a schedule, an app — and how did it help you?
  • Why is a small, strictly-followed repetition system more powerful than lots of variety?
Lesson objectives
  • Explain how repetition creates unity and brand recognition across a design or design system
  • Explain how proximity creates meaning by grouping related elements — the Gestalt principle of proximity
  • Diagnose proximity failures where spatial grouping contradicts logical relationships
  • Build a simple repetition system — consistent colors, type, spacing, and shape language
  • Apply both principles together to organize a complex information layout into clear, meaningful groups
Key vocabulary
  • Proximity — the Gestalt rule that near elements read as related and distant ones as unrelated — structure from space alone.
  • Repetition — consistent reuse of colors, type, spacing, and shapes — unity within a design, recognition across a system.
  • Intra-group Spacing — the tight space within a group, signaling its elements belong together.
  • Inter-group Spacing — the wide space between groups, signaling they are distinct.
  • Gestalt Grouping — the perceptual principles by which the brain clusters elements by similarity, proximity, and continuity.
  • Repetition System — a small fixed set of decisions — typefaces, colors, spacing, shape language — applied everywhere for unity.
