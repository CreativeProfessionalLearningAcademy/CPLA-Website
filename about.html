About the Academy

What is CPLA?

CPLA is a professional learning academy, not a course library. Illustrator, Photoshop, and InDesign are taught alongside the creative thinking, judgment, communication, and workflow discipline the work actually requires — never in isolation, and never as an end in themselves.

Every lesson is built around a professional problem, not a feature tour. Students don't practice the Pen tool for its own sake — they use it to solve a client brief, hit a real production constraint, and deliver a file the way a working designer would hand it off. The software is the instrument. The outcome we're building toward is the professional.

Build creative professionals who are prepared to contribute with competence, confidence, and professionalism from day one.

Educational Philosophy

How we teach.

Software changes. Professional thinking does not.

The creative industry rewards people who solve problems, communicate clearly, manage projects, and consistently produce quality work — not people who can recite a menu of keyboard shortcuts. CPLA teaches software as an essential tool, never as the end goal.

CPLA follows an apprenticeship-inspired model. Students learn by observing, practicing, receiving feedback, refining their work, and gradually taking on more complex professional challenges — the same way a junior designer grows inside a working studio. Lessons build cumulatively across the curriculum: nothing is taught in isolation, and every new skill strengthens the ones that came before it.

How Work Gets Assessed

Real feedback, every submission.

Most software courses grade completion — did you finish the exercise. CPLA grades work, against the specific learning objectives of that exact lesson, every single time. Every assignment is scored against a rubric built from its own objectives, not a generic checklist recycled across the curriculum.

A grade tells you where you landed. Feedback tells you how to get further.

When a student submits a project, it's reviewed against that lesson's rubric using CPLA's AI Design Review System, built on Anthropic's Claude. The review returns a criterion-by-criterion breakdown, specific strengths, specific areas to improve, and a note comparing the submission to the student's prior work — so growth is visible, not just a score.

Given the volume of work a program like this generates, the AI Design Review is the primary evaluator for most submissions — a deliberate design choice, not a limitation. Every review applies the same rubric, the same criteria, the same way, every time — a consistency no grader working through a stack of a hundred projects a week can fully guarantee. Instructors stay in the loop to spot-check and resolve edge cases, but the rubric-anchored review is the standard assessment, not a placeholder for one.

Where It Leads

A two-year path, not three classes.

CPLA's seven-volume curriculum is Year One of a two-year professional pathway — the complete foundation, not a stand-alone product built around a handful of courses. Year Two is where the platform's real purpose takes shape.

Year One
Foundations
The full seven-volume curriculum — design fundamentals through Illustrator, Photoshop, InDesign, and After Effects — taught through real professional problems and held to the same rubric-based standard from the first lesson to the last.
Year Two
Professional Practice
Students step out of structured lessons and work as junior designers on real projects, building toward a formal two-stage capstone.
Field Rehearsal → Master Client Capstone → Certificate of Completion
The capstone isn't a final exam. It's a client with a real deadline and a real opinion.

The Field Rehearsal comes first — a lower-stakes, single-deliverable engagement with an actual client, run through a full discovery-to-approval cycle. It's where students make their rookie mistakes on a small project, before those mistakes matter on the capstone.

The Master Client Capstone

The terminal project is a complete, real-client engagement that has to prove competency across all seven volumes at once — not just the most recent one.
